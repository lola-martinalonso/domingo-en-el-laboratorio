Expanded PTFE, better known as GORE-TEX, was discovered unexpectedly in 1969 by Bob Gore after sharply stretching PTFE (polytetrafluoroethylene). It marked a before and after in many fields. **NASA** chose it for the Columbia astronauts on their journey to the Moon. In 1990, the members of an international expedition to **Antarctica** did not hesitate to take GORE-TEX outer garments to cross the frozen continent; one of the explorers said the laminate saved his life.

It had become the material with the best performance in the most extreme conditions. But they did not stop there: they developed new product technologies — PACLITE, XCR, Pro, SURROUND, SHAKEDRY, Invisible Fit, INFINIUM — that multiply a garment's performance according to the conditions it will face.

## How can a membrane be waterproof and breathable at once?

Rainwater is **liquid**, while sweat is a **gas**. A water molecule is thousands of times larger than a molecule of sweat. If the pores are small enough, water cannot get through while sweat has a far easier time. And so it is: the pore is **20,000 times smaller than a drop of water** and yet **700 times larger than a sweat molecule**.

> If you compare a drop of water to the Earth, the pore in the membrane would be a football pitch, with the ball being the size of the sweat molecule.

But it is not that simple. In PTFE, every carbon atom in the polymer backbone is fully bonded to fluorine atoms. The carbon-fluorine bond is so strong that neither oxygen nor ultraviolet light has enough energy to break it, and the material's good properties come from that strength: it has been shown to last more than a hundred years outdoors and to withstand temperatures from **-240 to +280 degrees**.

The distribution of fluorine around the carbon backbone balances the charges, which makes PTFE non-polar. Non-polar materials are not attractive to polar substances such as water. This fully fluorinated polymer also has low surface energy. Water molecules, by contrast, have very high surface tension and tend to cluster together. Little attraction to the material plus strong attraction to each other means large separated droplets form, bigger than the pore. That same low tension drives breathability: the moment water vapour touches the ePTFE, it is pushed out through the pores.

## The problem of a contaminated membrane

The fabric was found to lose its properties with use. Body oils, dirt, sweat, sunscreen and hand creams all have higher surface tension than ePTFE; they contaminate the membrane and block its pores. A contaminated membrane turns from water-repellent to hydrophilic: something that attracts water molecules instead of repelling them.

Outside, the problem was solved with a **DWR** (durable water repellent) treatment, which creates a rough zigzag surface that stops water spreading and forces it into round droplets on the peaks. Inside, where the real problem was, an **ultra-thin oleophobic polyurethane** layer was added to protect the membrane: a PU sponge that collects the moisture the body generates and moves it outwards.

- **Absorption.** Sweat molecules are drawn to the PU layer thanks to its hydrophilicity, soaking it.
- **Diffusion.** With a difference in moisture concentration between the layer and the membrane, moisture travels from the higher concentration to the lower.
- **Desorption.** Water vapour leaves the ePTFE membrane and escapes outside.

Think of dipping part of a paper napkin into spilled water: the water immediately travels from the wet areas to the dry ones, soaking the whole napkin. There it meets the hydrophobicity of the ePTFE, which pushes the moisture out through the pores as vapour. Besides the layer and the membrane, an inner textile lining was added to protect the assembly and make it pleasant to the touch.

## Waterproof, breathable, windproof

The thermal conductivity of a wet fabric is three times that of a dry one: that is why wet fabric draws body heat. GORE-TEX garments keep water out and therefore stop heat loss.

If cold wind passes through a garment, it causes convective heat loss and destroys the thin layer of warm air between body and clothing. GORE-TEX fabrics keep the wind out thanks to their networked structure of nodes and fibres. The result is a comfortable body microclimate.

Finally, when the body is moving the skin sheds excess heat through sweat. To stop it building up, the membrane helps transfer it outwards.

One last detail: every stitch in a seam is a potential leak. That is why all seams in GORE-TEX garments, footwear and gloves are heat-sealed with GORE-SEAM tape in approved factories.
